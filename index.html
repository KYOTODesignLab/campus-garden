Workshop

XR Garden

Design & Construction

A workshop using extended reality to study the position and form of the garden, connecting those decisions with its on-site design and construction.

Design and spatial studies

Positions, forms, and boundaries were tested against the physical site through the headset overlay, ahead of any fixed drawing.

On-site process

The overlay was walked and checked against the ground itself, so decisions made in the headset could be read directly against the site.

Construction

Construction followed from the studies above, carrying the positions and forms fixed on site into the built garden.

Documentation

Records from the workshop — plates, scans, and site notes — are gathered into the wider Archive as they are prepared.

Campus Garden

Gathering Information

An ongoing workshop observing the completed garden and gathering information about its plants, maintenance, spaces, and activities across different forms of record.

Observation and recording

The garden’s conditions, activities, and changes over time are recorded through observation on site.

Plants and maintenance

Plant species, locations, conditions, and methods of care are organised as information for maintaining the garden.

Maps, point clouds, and photography document the garden’s position, form, and changing conditions.

About

A garden is never finished.

Campus Garden is an experimental garden at Kyoto Institute of Technology. Students, gardeners, faculty, and visitors continue to shape it through making, use, care, and observation.

The project brings hands-on knowledge together with digital tools — construction, environmental sensing, photography, mapping, and 3D recording. Each activity and record is shared to deepen understanding of the garden and support what happens next.

Place

A courtyard garden on the Matsugasaki campus, Kyoto Institute of Technology.

Project

An experimental garden held as a living, shared record rather than a finished result.

Workshop

Developed across phases: design & construction, then archive, maintenance, and connection.

Methods

Body-led judgement joined with XR, sensing, photography, mapping, and 3D scanning.
